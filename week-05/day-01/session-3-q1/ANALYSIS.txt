Sentiment Analysis with Stacked LSTMs

This experiment trained a binary sentiment classifier on 10,000 IMDB reviews,
comprising 5,000 positive and 5,000 negative examples. Validation used 2,000
reviews with equal class representation. Reviews were selected in sorted filename
order. A tokenizer fitted exclusively on training text limited the vocabulary to
10,000 indices. Sequences were post-padded or post-truncated to 200 tokens, and
zero padding was masked. NumPy and TensorFlow used seed 42.

The network combined a 128-dimensional embedding with stacked LSTM layers of
128 and 64 units. The first recurrent layer returned sequences for the second.
Three dropout layers, each with rate 0.2, provided regularization. A 64-unit ReLU
layer preceded the sigmoid output. Adam optimized binary cross-entropy for ten
epochs using batches of 128. The model contained 1,465,217 trainable parameters.

Training accuracy increased from 70.76% in epoch one to 98.68% in epoch ten,
while training loss decreased from 0.5529 to 0.0446. Validation accuracy initially
reached 80.60%, peaked at 83.50% in epoch five, and finished at 80.90%. Final
validation loss was 0.8190, compared with its minimum of 0.4252 in epoch two.
The final accuracy gap was 17.78 percentage points. These measurements come
from the completed run, rather than the illustrative figures in the instructions.

The widening gap indicates overfitting: additional training improved performance
on known examples without delivering sustained validation gains. Rising
cross-entropy despite broadly stable validation accuracy also suggests increasingly
confident incorrect predictions. Validation accuracy fluctuated, including a drop
to 73.00% in epoch three, so a single intermediate result should not determine
conclusions. The saved chart shows both trajectories and their epoch-to-epoch
variation, with final accuracy values in the bottom annotation.

Future experiments could compare early stopping based on validation loss,
smaller recurrent layers, stronger regularization, and more training reviews.
These alternatives were not applied here because the requested run specifies
ten epochs and a fixed architecture. Truncation can discard sentiment-bearing
text late in longer reviews, while excluded vocabulary can remove informative
rare words. Finally, the assignment's test split was monitored during training;
it therefore serves as validation rather than an untouched final evaluation.
A separate held-out set and repeated seeded runs would support stronger claims
about generalization before deployment.
